World models

A world model predicts what happens next: given a state and an action, which state follows and what reward arrives. With one, an agent can plan by imagining trajectories instead of living them. Dyna-Q (Lesson 59) remembered single transitions. Here the agent learns the probabilities, then solves the whole maze in its head.

The maths

The simplest world model is a table of counts. With $N(s, a)$ the times action $a$ was taken in $s$ and $N(s, a, s')$ the times it led to $s'$:

$$\hat p(s' \mid s, a) = \frac{N(s, a, s')}{N(s, a)}$$

and $\hat r(s, a)$ is the average reward seen after taking $a$ in $s$.

These are maximum-likelihood estimates. By the law of large numbers they converge to the true $p$ and $r$ as each pair is tried more often (Lesson 6's averages again).

Planning then treats the model as if it were the truth and runs value iteration (Lesson 33) on it:

$$V(s) \leftarrow \max_a \sum_{s'} \hat p(s' \mid s, a) \big[ \hat r(s, a) + \gamma V(s') \big]$$

Every backup is imagined. No real step is spent. The policy is only as good as the model: where $\hat p$ is wrong or missing, the plan is confidently wrong. Explore 50 steps, plan and act. Then explore more and plan again.

Modern world models learn the same two things with networks instead of tables. Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) compressed game frames with a variational autoencoder, learned a recurrent model of how the compressed state evolves, and trained a controller entirely inside that "dream". MuZero (Schrittwieser et al., 2020) goes further. Its model never predicts observations. From a hidden state $s^k$ it predicts only what planning needs:

$$\begin{aligned} s^0 &= h_\theta(o_t) &&\text{(encode the observation)} \\ r^k, s^k &= g_\theta(s^{k-1}, a^k) &&\text{(imagined step)} \\ \mathbf p^k, v^k &= f_\theta(s^k) &&\text{(policy and value, as in Lesson 96)} \end{aligned}$$

It is trained so that rewards, values and policies predicted along imagined trajectories match those seen in real games. Lesson 96's tree search then runs inside this learned model, which let MuZero match AlphaZero at chess, shogi and Go without being told the rules.
