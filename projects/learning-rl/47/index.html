Driving home

You predict how long the drive home will take, and revise the prediction as things happen. Monte Carlo can only correct the early predictions once you are home. TD corrects each prediction the moment the next one is made. This is S&B's Example 6.1.

A new office

You move to a new office with a new car park, but join the same highway (S&B Exercise 6.2). The highway and later parts of the drive already have accurate predictions. The two new states start at a guess of 30 minutes to go; the true values are 46 and 38. Each trip's segment times are random, and the highway is the noisiest. Error is the RMS error of the two new predictions, averaged over 500 people learning in parallel.

The maths

The state is where you are in the drive. The value $V(S_t)$ is the predicted time still to go. The "reward" is the time each leg takes, and there is no discounting ($\gamma = 1$), so the return $G_t$ is the actual time to go.

$$\text{MC:}\quad V(S_t) \leftarrow V(S_t) + \alpha\big[G_t - V(S_t)\big]$$

Monte Carlo moves each prediction towards the time that was actually still to go. It needs $G_t$, so nothing can change until you are home.

$$\text{TD:}\quad V(S_t) \leftarrow V(S_t) + \alpha\big[R_{t+1} + V(S_{t+1}) - V(S_t)\big]$$

TD moves each prediction towards the time the next leg took plus the next prediction. That is known one event later.

Worked example, leaving the office with $\alpha = 1$. The prediction was 30 minutes. Reaching the car took 5 minutes and the new prediction there was 35 to go:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{TD target} &= 5 + 35 = 40, & \text{change} &= 40 - 30 = +10 \\ \text{MC target} &= 43, & \text{change} &= 43 - 30 = +13 \end{aligned}$$

TD hears about the rain within 5 minutes. MC has to wait 43.

Lesson 46's identity links them. With the predictions held fixed, the MC change is the sum of the TD changes from that point on: for leaving the office, $10 - 5 + 5 + 3 + 0 = 13$.
