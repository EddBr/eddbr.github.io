Policy evaluation

The Bellman equation says what $v_\pi$ must satisfy. Policy evaluation turns it into an update: start from any guess, apply the equation to every state, and repeat. Each sweep pushes information one more step out from the terminal states.

The maths

From Lesson 26, $v_\pi$ satisfies the Bellman expectation equation for every state $s$:

$$v_\pi(s) = \sum_a \pi(a \mid s) \sum_{s', r} p(s', r \mid s, a)\,\big[r + \gamma\, v_\pi(s')\big]$$

That is one equation per state. Lesson 28 solved them with a matrix inverse, which costs about $|\mathcal{S}|^3$ operations. Iterative policy evaluation uses the right-hand side as an update instead:

$$v_{k+1}(s) = \sum_a \pi(a \mid s) \sum_{s', r} p(s', r \mid s, a)\,\big[r + \gamma\, v_k(s')\big]$$

The new value of $s$ is the expected one-step reward plus the discounted old value of wherever you land. Terminal states stay at $0$. This kind of update is called an expected update: it averages over every possible next state using the model $p$, rather than a sample.

Why it converges, for $\gamma < 1$. Subtract the Bellman equation from the update. The rewards cancel:

$$\begin{aligned} |v_{k+1}(s) - v_\pi(s)| &= \Big|\gamma \sum_a \pi(a \mid s) \sum_{s', r} p(s', r \mid s, a)\,\big[v_k(s') - v_\pi(s')\big]\Big| \\ &\le \gamma \max_{s'} |v_k(s') - v_\pi(s')| \end{aligned}$$

A weighted average can't exceed its largest term. So each sweep shrinks the worst error by at least a factor $\gamma$, and $\max_s |v_k(s) - v_\pi(s)| \le \gamma^k \max_s |v_0(s) - v_\pi(s)|$.

In the gridworld $\gamma = 1$, and the argument above gives nothing. It still converges because the random policy reaches a corner with probability 1 from every cell (S&B §4.1 states the condition). The chart shows the error falling at a steady rate on the log scale: geometric convergence.

In practice the loop stops when the largest change in a sweep, $\Delta = \max_s |v_{k+1}(s) - v_k(s)|$, falls below a small threshold $\theta$.
