Averages, one step at a time

An agent that pulls a lever a million times shouldn't store a million rewards. It can keep the average up to date with just two numbers: the current estimate and a count.

The maths

Let $Q_n$ be the estimate after $n-1$ rewards. Then $Q_{n+1}$ is the average of the first $n$:

$$\begin{aligned} Q_{n+1} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} R_i = \frac{1}{n}\Big(R_n + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} R_i\Big) \\ &= \frac{1}{n}\big(R_n + (n-1)\,Q_n\big) \\ &= Q_n + \frac{1}{n}\big(R_n - Q_n\big) \end{aligned}$$

Only $Q_n$ and $n$ are needed. The last line has a shape you will see in almost every lesson from here on:

$$\text{New} \leftarrow \text{Old} + \text{StepSize}\,\big[\text{Target} - \text{Old}\big]$$

$\text{Target} - \text{Old}$ is the error. Each update moves the estimate a fraction of the way towards the target. In the interactive, the blue bar is the error and the green part is the step actually taken.

Notice the first step: with $n = 1$ the step size is $1$, so $Q_2 = R_1$ whatever $Q_1$ was.
