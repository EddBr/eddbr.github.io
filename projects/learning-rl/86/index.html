From preferences to scores

RL needs a number for each reply, but people are bad at giving consistent scores out of 10. They are much better at saying which of two replies is better. The Bradley–Terry model turns those comparisons into scores.

Which answer is better?

Prompt: "Explain why the sky is blue to a ten-year-old."

Scores start at 0 and are shown with their mean subtracted. Each choice is one step of size $K = 0.5$. The simulated rater has its own hidden scores and picks the winner with Bradley–Terry probability, so it is sometimes inconsistent, like real people.

The maths

Give each answer $i$ a score $r_i$. The Bradley–Terry model (1952) says

$$P(i \succ j) = \frac{e^{r_i}}{e^{r_i} + e^{r_j}} = \sigma(r_i - r_j), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$$

$i \succ j$ means "$i$ is preferred to $j$". Only the gap between scores matters. Equal scores give $0.5$; a gap of $\log 9 \approx 2.2$ gives $0.9$.

Only differences are defined. Adding the same constant $c$ to every score changes nothing, since $(r_i + c) - (r_j + c) = r_i - r_j$. That is why the bars are centred.

Fitting the scores. Given a comparison where $w$ beat $l$, the log-likelihood is $\log \sigma(r_w - r_l)$. Using $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))$,

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial r_w} \log \sigma(r_w - r_l) &= 1 - \sigma(r_w - r_l) \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial r_l} \log \sigma(r_w - r_l) &= -\big(1 - \sigma(r_w - r_l)\big) \end{aligned}$$

One gradient ascent step with step size $K$ is the update used above:

$$\begin{aligned} r_w &\leftarrow r_w + K\big(1 - \sigma(r_w - r_l)\big) \\ r_l &\leftarrow r_l - K\big(1 - \sigma(r_w - r_l)\big) \end{aligned}$$

The winner gains what the model failed to predict. An expected win moves the scores a little; an upset moves them a lot. It is the old-plus-step-times-error shape from Lesson 6 again, with target 1 and prediction $\sigma(r_w - r_l)$.

Elo. Chess ratings use the same model in different units: $P(i \succ j) = 1 / (1 + 10^{(R_j - R_i)/400})$, which is $\sigma\big((R_i - R_j)\ln 10 / 400\big)$. A 400-point gap means 10-to-1 odds.
