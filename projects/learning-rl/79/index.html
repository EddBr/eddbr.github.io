Continuous actions

A value-based agent needs $\max_a Q(s,a)$, which is awkward when the action is a real number such as a force or an angle. A policy-gradient agent only needs to sample actions and compute $\nabla \ln \pi$. A Gaussian policy does both easily, and it learns its own amount of exploration through $\sigma$.

Aim a throw

The action $a$ is where the ball lands, in metres. The policy is $a \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$: the curve is its density. The reward is $R = e^{-(a - x^*)^2 / 2}$: 1 for a throw into the bucket at $x^*$, falling off with distance. Click the ground to move the bucket.

"Learn" runs REINFORCE with a baseline, one throw per update, and moves the sliders itself. Start far from the bucket: $\sigma$ first grows, because a wider spread is the only way to land anywhere near it. Once $\mu$ arrives, $\sigma$ shrinks and the throws tighten. With the bucket under $\mu$, $J$ keeps rising as $\sigma$ falls, so $\sigma$ heads for 0; it is kept above 0.1, and learning stops after 1500 throws.

The maths

For a real-valued action, the policy is a probability density. S&B §13.7 uses a normal distribution whose mean and standard deviation are functions of the state:

$$\pi(a \mid s, \boldsymbol\theta) = \frac{1}{\sigma(s,\boldsymbol\theta)\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\!\Big(-\frac{(a - \mu(s,\boldsymbol\theta))^2}{2\sigma(s,\boldsymbol\theta)^2}\Big)$$

with $\mu(s,\boldsymbol\theta) = \boldsymbol\theta_\mu^\top \mathbf{x}_\mu(s)$ and $\sigma(s,\boldsymbol\theta) = \exp\big(\boldsymbol\theta_\sigma^\top \mathbf{x}_\sigma(s)\big)$. The exponential keeps $\sigma$ positive. A density can exceed 1; only its integral must equal 1. Here there is one state and $\mathbf{x} = 1$, so $\mu = \theta_\mu$ and $\sigma = e^{\theta_\sigma}$.

Taking the log, $\ln \pi = -\theta_\sigma - \tfrac12 \ln 2\pi - (a-\mu)^2 e^{-2\theta_\sigma}/2$. Differentiating:

$$\frac{\partial \ln \pi}{\partial \theta_\mu} = \frac{a - \mu}{\sigma^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial \ln \pi}{\partial \theta_\sigma} = \frac{(a - \mu)^2}{\sigma^2} - 1$$

A throw beyond $\mu$ points the mean gradient forwards. A throw more than one $\sigma$ from $\mu$ points the spread gradient towards wider; a closer throw points it towards narrower. Multiply by $R - b$ and these become the REINFORCE-with-baseline updates from Lesson 74, unchanged.

This task's $J$. With $R = e^{-(a-x^*)^2/(2w^2)}$ ($w = 1$ here) and $a \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, a Gaussian integral gives

$$J(\mu, \sigma) = \frac{w}{\sqrt{w^2 + \sigma^2}} \exp\!\Big(-\frac{(\mu - x^*)^2}{2(w^2 + \sigma^2)}\Big)$$

Its derivative in $\sigma$ has the sign of $(\mu - x^*)^2 - (w^2 + \sigma^2)$. Far from the bucket, widening helps; close to it, narrowing helps. That is the "$\sigma$ should" readout, and the path the learner takes.
