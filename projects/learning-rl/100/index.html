Final review

Ninety-nine lessons, one recurring move: nudge an estimate towards a target, $\text{New} \leftarrow \text{Old} + \alpha[\text{Target} - \text{Old}]$ (Lesson 6). The methods differ mainly in what the target is. This page lays them out on one map, races them on one maze, and lists the ideas worth keeping.

The map of value methods

Every value method backs up a target built from what comes next. Two questions place it: does the target use one sampled next step or an expectation over all of them (width), and does it bootstrap after one step or wait for the end of the episode (depth)? The maths

The course in four equations. A return discounts future rewards (Lesson 4):

$$\begin{aligned} G_t &= R_{t+1} + \gamma R_{t+2} + \gamma^2 R_{t+3} + \dots \\ &= R_{t+1} + \gamma G_{t+1} \end{aligned}$$

Values are expected returns, and the recursion in $G_t$ gives the Bellman equations (Lessons 26 and 29):

$$v_\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[R_{t+1} + \gamma v_\pi(S_{t+1}) \mid S_t = s\big]$$ $$\begin{aligned} q_*(s, a) = \mathbb{E}\big[&R_{t+1} + \gamma \max_{a'} q_*(S_{t+1}, a') \\ &\mid S_t = s, A_t = a\big] \end{aligned}$$

Learning from samples replaces the expectation with one transition and moves part of the way, driven by the TD error (Lesson 46):

$$\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma V(S_{t+1}) - V(S_t)$$ $$V(S_t) \leftarrow V(S_t) + \alpha\, \delta_t$$

And policy methods climb the gradient of expected return directly, weighting each action's log-probability by how much better it was than expected (Lessons 71 to 76):

$$\begin{aligned} &\nabla_\theta J(\theta) \\ &\quad \propto \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(A_t \mid S_t)\, A_\pi(S_t, A_t)\big] \end{aligned}$$

Here $A_\pi(s, a) = q_\pi(s, a) - v_\pi(s)$ is the advantage, and the expectation is over states and actions met while following $\pi_\theta$ (the $\propto$ hides a constant, as in S&B §13.2).

Everything else in the course is about making these work: exploring enough (Units 2 and 3), scaling with function approximation (Units 13 and 14), keeping policy steps safe (Unit 17), and choosing a reward that means what you intend (Unit 19).

Ideas worth keeping

  1. The agent optimises the reward you wrote, not the goal you meant. L2, L90
  2. Never trying is never learning. Every method needs a plan for exploration. L3, L9, L94
  3. Today's value is today's reward plus the discounted value of where you land. L25
  4. Bootstrapping lowers variance at the cost of some bias. Monte Carlo waits for the truth; TD guesses from a guess. L48
  5. Evaluate, improve, repeat. Almost every control method is generalised policy iteration. L34
  6. On-policy values include the agent's own mistakes; off-policy values assume it stops making them. L53
  7. A max over noisy estimates is biased upwards. L55, L69
  8. Bootstrapping, function approximation and off-policy data together can diverge. L65
  9. Subtract a baseline: it changes nothing in expectation and removes a lot of noise. L74, L93
  10. A learned model or reward is only right near its data. Optimise far from it and it lies. L91, L98
