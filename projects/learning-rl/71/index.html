The policy gradient

Gradient ascent on $J(\boldsymbol\theta)$ needs $\nabla J$. Its formula says: push up each action that beats the policy's average, and push harder for actions the policy plays often. The agent cannot compute that sum, but one sampled action and its reward give an estimate that is right on average.

The maths

One state first. With softmax preferences $\theta_b$, the expected reward is

$$J(\boldsymbol\theta) = \sum_a \pi(a)\, q(a)$$

The softmax derivative from Lesson 13 is $\partial \pi(a)/\partial \theta_b = \pi(a)\big(\mathbb{1}_{a=b} - \pi(b)\big)$, so

$$\frac{\partial J}{\partial \theta_b} = \sum_a q(a)\,\pi(a)\big(\mathbb{1}_{a=b} - \pi(b)\big) = \pi(b)\big(q(b) - J\big)$$

An action better than average ($q(b) > J$) gets its preference pushed up, and the push is larger when the action is played often. That is the green arrow.

The middle sum is an average over $A \sim \pi$, and $\mathbb{E}[R \mid A] = q(A)$. So one action and its reward give an unbiased sample of the gradient:

$$\frac{\partial J}{\partial \theta_b} = \mathbb{E}\Big[R\,\big(\mathbb{1}_{A=b} - \pi(b)\big)\Big]$$

Each blue line is one such sample. "Unbiased" means their average converges to the true gradient.

Many states. For an episodic MDP with $J(\boldsymbol\theta) = v_{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(s_0)$, the policy gradient theorem (S&B §13.2) gives

$$\nabla J(\boldsymbol\theta) \propto \sum_s \mu(s) \sum_a q_\pi(s,a)\, \nabla \pi(a \mid s, \boldsymbol\theta)$$

$\mu(s)$ is the fraction of time spent in $s$ under $\pi$. Changing $\boldsymbol\theta$ also changes which states are visited, but the theorem needs no derivative of $\mu$: weight each state by how often it is visited, and in it push every action's probability by that action's value. Since $\mu$ is the on-policy distribution, the sum is an expectation over the states the agent actually meets:

$$\nabla J(\boldsymbol\theta) \propto \mathbb{E}_\pi\Big[\sum_a q_\pi(S_t, a)\, \nabla \pi(a \mid S_t, \boldsymbol\theta)\Big]$$

Lesson 72 turns the inner sum into a single sampled action, and Lesson 73 replaces $q_\pi$ with the sampled return $G_t$. The result is $\nabla J \propto \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[G_t \nabla \ln \pi(A_t \mid S_t, \boldsymbol\theta)\big]$.
