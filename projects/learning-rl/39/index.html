Importance sampling

Sometimes the data comes from one policy but you want the value of another: learning the greedy policy while exploring, or judging a new policy from old logs. Importance sampling reweights each outcome by how much more (or less) likely the target policy was to produce it.

You can only roll the fair die $b$. You want the average roll of the loaded die $\pi$. Each roll $x$ gets weight $\rho = \pi(x)/b(x)$.

The maths

For one roll, reweighting turns an average under $b$ into an average under $\pi$:

$$\mathbb{E}_b\!\left[\frac{\pi(X)}{b(X)}\,X\right] = \sum_x b(x)\,\frac{\pi(x)}{b(x)}\,x = \sum_x \pi(x)\,x = \mathbb{E}_\pi[X]$$

This needs coverage: $b(x) > 0$ wherever $\pi(x) > 0$, or some outcomes can never be seen.

In an episode, the ratio multiplies over every action from time $t$ to the end $T$:

$$\rho_{t:T-1} = \prod_{k=t}^{T-1} \frac{\pi(A_k \mid S_k)\, p(S_{k+1} \mid S_k, A_k)}{b(A_k \mid S_k)\, p(S_{k+1} \mid S_k, A_k)} = \prod_{k=t}^{T-1} \frac{\pi(A_k \mid S_k)}{b(A_k \mid S_k)}$$

The transition probabilities appear in both the numerator and the denominator and cancel, so the ratio needs no model. Then $\mathbb{E}_b[\rho_{t:T-1} G_t \mid S_t = s] = v_\pi(s)$.

Let $\mathcal{T}(s)$ be the times $s$ is visited (first visits, across all episodes). Two ways to average:

$$V_{\text{ordinary}}(s) = \frac{\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}(s)} \rho_{t:T(t)-1}\, G_t}{|\mathcal{T}(s)|} \qquad V_{\text{weighted}}(s) = \frac{\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}(s)} \rho_{t:T(t)-1}\, G_t}{\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}(s)} \rho_{t:T(t)-1}}$$

Ordinary is unbiased, but its variance can be huge: one roll of 6 with $\rho = 3$ triples a value. Weighted is a weighted average of real returns, so it never leaves their range. It is biased (after one sample it just equals that return), but the bias fades and its variance is far lower. In practice weighted is preferred.

In the blackjack example, $\pi$ is deterministic and $b = \tfrac12$, so $\rho$ is $2^k$ if the behaviour policy happened to make the target's choice at all $k$ decisions, and $0$ otherwise.
