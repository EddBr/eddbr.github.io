Cliff walking

SARSA and Q-learning differ by one term. On a cliff edge that term decides everything. Q-learning learns the shortest path, right along the edge, and keeps falling off while it explores. SARSA learns a path that is safe for an agent that sometimes moves at random.

The maths

The two updates differ only in how they value the next state:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SARSA:}\quad & R_{t+1} + \gamma\, Q(S_{t+1}, A_{t+1}) \\ \text{Q-learning:}\quad & R_{t+1} + \gamma \max_a Q(S_{t+1}, a) \end{aligned}$$

So they learn different things. With an ε-greedy policy $\pi$, SARSA's values satisfy the Bellman equation for $q_\pi$, and Q-learning's satisfy the optimality equation for $q_*$:

$$\begin{aligned} q_\pi(s, a) &= \mathbb{E}\big[R_{t+1} + \gamma \textstyle\sum_{a'} \pi(a' \mid S_{t+1})\, q_\pi(S_{t+1}, a')\big] \\ q_*(s, a) &= \mathbb{E}\big[R_{t+1} + \gamma \max_{a'} q_*(S_{t+1}, a')\big] \end{aligned}$$

(Both expectations are conditioned on $S_t = s, A_t = a$.) $q_\pi$ includes the cost of the agent's own random moves, $q_*$ assumes perfect play from the next step on. Next to the cliff, $q_\pi$ is low, because a random "down" is a $-100$ fall. $q_*$ does not see that risk.

The risk is real while exploring. On 11 of the edge path's 13 steps, one action leads into the cliff: "right" from S, then "down" from each of the 10 edge cells. ε-greedy picks that action at random with probability $\varepsilon/4$, so the chance of falling at least once on a trip is

$$1 - \Big(1 - \frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big)^{11} = 1 - 0.975^{11} \approx 0.24 \quad (\varepsilon = 0.1)$$

Roughly one trip in four costs an extra 100 or more. That is why Q-learning's online reward per episode is worse even though its greedy path (13 steps, return $-13$) is optimal. SARSA's path along the top row takes 17 steps ($-17$) but almost never falls.

Neither is wrong. Q-learning values the agent as if it will stop exploring. SARSA values it as it actually behaves, random moves included. If $\varepsilon$ decays to 0, the two coincide and both find the 13-step path.
