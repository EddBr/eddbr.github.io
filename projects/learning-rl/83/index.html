The clipped objective

Lesson 82's surrogate $r\hat A$ rewards pushing the ratio as far as it will go. PPO caps the reward: once $r$ leaves $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$ in the direction the advantage wants, that sample stops contributing gradient. The policy can be trained for many epochs on one batch without running away.

The maths

With $r_t(\theta)$ the probability ratio and $\hat A_t$ the advantage estimate, PPO maximises (Schulman et al. 2017, eq. 7)

$$\begin{aligned} L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t\Big[\min\big(&r_t(\theta)\,\hat A_t,\\ &\operatorname{clip}\big(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon\big)\,\hat A_t\big)\Big] \end{aligned}$$

$\operatorname{clip}(r, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)$ forces $r$ into that interval. The paper uses $\epsilon = 0.2$.

Two cases. Taking the minimum works out as

$$\min\big(r\hat A, \operatorname{clip}(r)\hat A\big) = \begin{cases} \min(r,\, 1+\epsilon)\,\hat A & \hat A > 0 \\ \max(r,\, 1-\epsilon)\,\hat A & \hat A < 0 \end{cases}$$

A good action ($\hat A > 0$) earns credit for being made more likely, up to $r = 1 + \epsilon$ and no further. A bad action ($\hat A < 0$) earns credit for being made less likely, down to $r = 1 - \epsilon$. On the flat parts the gradient with respect to $\theta$ is zero, so that sample stops pushing.

Mistakes are never clipped. If a bad action became more likely ($\hat A < 0$, $r > 1 + \epsilon$) or a good one less likely ($\hat A > 0$, $r < 1 - \epsilon$), the minimum picks $r\hat A$ with its full gradient, pulling $r$ back. The minimum makes $L^{\text{CLIP}}$ a pessimistic version of $L^{\text{CPI}}$:

$$L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) \le L^{\text{CPI}}(\theta)$$

The two are equal, with equal gradients, at $\theta = \theta_{\text{old}}$. Near $r = 1$ nothing is clipped, so the first step is the ordinary policy gradient. Further steps can only be credited inside the trust region.

The algorithm. Repeat: run $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$ to collect a batch; compute $\hat A_t$ with GAE (Lesson 78); do several epochs of minibatch gradient ascent on $L^{\text{CLIP}}$; set $\theta_{\text{old}} \leftarrow \theta$.

The clip removes the incentive to move, not the movement itself. Other samples, and the shared softmax, can still carry a ratio past $1 \pm \epsilon$, as the $r$ column sometimes shows. In practice the KL stays small, which is what the bound in Lesson 82 asks for.
