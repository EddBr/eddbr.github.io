    Specimens

    Rendered ahead of time with the same code, 384 steps each, and checked against Google's own copy of the network after MP3 encoding. Click one to hear it and load its settings, then press Generate to grow it yourself.

    The classifier

    YAMNet is Google's general-purpose sound tagger. It hears audio as a picture: a log-mel spectrogram, 0.96 seconds wide and 64 frequency bands tall, fed to the same MobileNet used for photos.

    NetworkYAMNet: MobileNet v1 on 96 × 64 log-mel patches, 3.7M parameters (Google, TF Models)
    Trained onAudioSet: about 2 million 10-second YouTube clips, labelled by people
    Outputs521 sound classes, each an independent yes/no (sigmoid)
    Front end25 ms Hann STFT every 10 ms → 64 mel bands, 125 Hz–7.5 kHz → log. Rebuilt here from matmuls so it can be differentiated.
    Download7.5 MB (fp16)

    The 1/f trick, on a spectrogram

    Change raw audio samples and the fastest way up is high-frequency fizz that only the network cares about. So the sound is stored as a spectrogram, and the spectrogram is stored the way the image version stores pixels: as 2-D Fourier coefficients scaled by 1/fa. Broad shapes in time and pitch are cheap, and per-cell speckle is expensive. Slide Smoothness to 0 to switch that off.

    Why it still sounds like static

    YAMNet only sees 64 blurry bands, about 10 ms apart. Anything finer is invisible to it, so the optimiser never has a reason to make clean pitches or sharp onsets. It just paints energy wherever the bands need it, and the result is buzzing, rustling texture with the right outline. The siren's V-shaped sweeps and the heartbeat's thumps are the outline showing through.

    A loop, heard from anywhere

    The spectrogram's Fourier coefficients wrap around in time, so the 4-second clip loops with no seam. Each step the network hears four overlapping 0.96 s windows starting from a random point in the loop, which is the audio version of jittering an image. The score shown is the average over every window once round the loop, with no jitter.
